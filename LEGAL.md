# Legal, privacy, and project policies

Last updated: **28 October 2026**.

DrainTracer is an open-source Windows diagnostic tool maintained by
[Junaid Ahamed](https://github.com/JunaidAhamed-7777). It runs on your computer
or runner. The project currently provides no hosted monitoring, report-storage,
account, subscription, or payment service.

## Find the right document

- [Privacy policy](PRIVACY.md): information processed locally, the automatic
  PyPI update request, voluntary communications, retention, and privacy requests.
- [Terms of use](TERMS.md): software licensing, authorized monitoring,
  operational risks, contributions, community participation, and disclaimers.
  These are also the project's terms of service for its current project
  resources; they do not describe a hosted service.
- [MIT license](LICENSE): the permissions and conditions for the software and
  accompanying documentation. It takes precedence over explanatory policies
  on software-license matters.
- [Third-party notices](THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md): dependency licenses and
  guidance for people distributing a copy or bundle.
- [Privacy and safe sharing guide](docs/privacy.md): report contents,
  redaction limits, network behavior, and practical storage and sharing steps.
- [Security policy](SECURITY.md): private vulnerability reporting, supported
  maintenance scope, and the tool's security boundaries.
- [Support](SUPPORT.md), [contributing](CONTRIBUTING.md), and
  [code of conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md): help, contribution requirements, and
  conduct reporting and enforcement.

## Contact

Use a private contact method published on the
[maintainer's GitHub profile](https://github.com/JunaidAhamed-7777) for privacy,
legal, licensing, or conduct concerns. If none is available, open an
[issue](https://github.com/JunaidAhamed-7777/DrainTracer/issues/new) titled
**Private contact requested** and ask only for a private channel. Do not put
personal data, credentials, allegations, or confidential evidence in that issue.
An issue is public, including its title and your GitHub identity.

For a vulnerability, follow [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md), which also explains
GitHub's private reporting option when it is enabled. For GitHub account or
platform data, use [GitHub's privacy contacts](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement#contact-us).
The maintainer cannot access or delete reports kept only on your machine.

## Scope and updates

These documents describe the official project and its current local software.
They do not govern a fork's separate services, your employer's monitoring
program, the target application's data practices, or third-party websites.
Hosting, a team service, a pilot study, or new data collection would need its
own factual review and appropriate notices before it starts. Roadmap entries
are proposals, not available services or contractual commitments.

Policy dates are independent of software release versions. Material policy
changes should identify their effective date and be recorded in repository
history; behavior changes also belong in the appropriate release notes.
Publishing documentation alone does not create a new software release or
change the license on copies already distributed.

## Maintainer review when behavior changes

Keep these documents aligned with the implementation. Review them when adding
a network destination, telemetry, uploads, new report fields, log capture,
accounts, a new contact route, or different retention behavior. Review
third-party notices when dependencies or bundled assets change. A new hosted
service needs specific decisions about its operator, jurisdictions, access,
processors, security, retention, deletion, and user rights before launch.

These project documents are not a certification of compliance with every
jurisdiction's laws. Obtain legal advice for the project's actual deployment
and obligations where needed; do not infer a data-processing agreement,
service-level agreement, or regulated-use approval from these documents.
